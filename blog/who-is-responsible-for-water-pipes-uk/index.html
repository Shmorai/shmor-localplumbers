Who is responsible for water pipes: you or your water company?

A wet patch in the garden or a drop in water pressure raises one question straight away: whose pipe is it? In the UK the answer depends on where the pipe sits, and getting it wrong can mean paying for a repair that was not yours, or waiting for a company that was never going to come. This guide sets out the usual split.

What your water company looks after

The Consumer Council for Water (CCW) explains that the water main is the pipe supplying the street with drinking water. It is owned by your water company, which is responsible for looking after it. The company is also responsible for:

  • any pipe connecting the main up to the point where it crosses your property boundary;
  • the external stop tap, usually in the public footpath; and
  • the water meter, if there is one.

Thames Water describes the pipe linking your supply pipe to its main as the communication pipe. Wording varies between companies, so check your own company's pipe-responsibility page.

What the homeowner looks after

According to CCW, the homeowner is responsible for water pipes on private property, from the boundary of the road where the company's main is laid. That includes the underground supply pipe running through your garden or drive to the house, and everything inside it: internal pipes, taps, toilets, tanks and appliances.

Thames Water adds two points worth knowing. First, a supply pipe that crosses someone else's land, such as a neighbour's property or the road, is still your responsibility. Second, once a leak on your property is confirmed, Thames Water says it is your legal responsibility to arrange the repair within four weeks. Other companies publish their own rules, so ask yours.

Shared pipes, tenants and landlords

On a terraced street, a single supply pipe sometimes serves several homes. CCW says that where a pipe serves more than one property, it is the joint responsibility of all the owners connected to it. Thames Water notes that it does not get involved in disputes between neighbours, so agree who will arrange the work and how the cost is split, preferably in writing.

If you rent, Thames Water says your landlord is responsible for fixing leaks. Tell the landlord or letting agent as soon as possible, in writing, and keep a copy.

How to work out where the leak is, and who to call

  1. Check inside first. Look for dripping taps, running toilets and wet patches. If the leak is inside, it is yours to fix.
  2. Turn off the water at your internal stop tap, and see our guide to finding your stop tap. If the leak stops, the fault is on your side of that tap.
  3. Find the boundary. A leak in the road or pavement should be reported to your water company. A leak in your garden or drive is normally on your supply pipe.
  4. Ask your water company. If you are not sure, contact them. Most publish pipe diagrams and take leak reports at any time.

If water is coming through a ceiling or reaching electrics, treat it as an emergency; our guide to leaks and burst pipes and the emergency plumbers page explain the first steps.

What to tell the plumber or water company

Whoever you call, you will get a faster and more accurate answer if you can describe the problem clearly. Before you phone, note:

  • Where you can see water: inside, in the garden, on the drive, or in the road or pavement.
  • When you first noticed it, and whether it is getting worse.
  • Whether the problem stops when you close your internal stop tap.
  • Whether you have a water meter, and any unusual rise in readings.
  • Whether you own or rent the property, and whether the pipe serves any neighbours.

Take photos of damp patches or pooling water. They help your insurer, your landlord and any contractor.

Choosing someone to repair a supply pipe

CCW points homeowners with leaks inside the home towards WaterSafe, the register of approved plumbers, via watersafe.org.uk. Supply pipe repairs are different work from fixing a tap, so ask any plumber whether they have done underground supply pipe replacements and get the scope and price in writing. Our guides to choosing a plumber and what affects plumbing costs help you compare quotes, and the plumber finder starts from your country and region.

Check your home insurance too: some policies cover escape of water or supply pipe damage, but terms vary, so read yours or ask your insurer before work begins.

This article covers the UK. In the USA the dividing line between utility and homeowner is set by your local water utility, so ask it before you hire anyone.

Frequently asked questions

Who is responsible for the water pipe in my garden?
Usually you. CCW says the homeowner looks after water pipes on private property up to the boundary of the road where the water main is laid. Your water company is responsible for the main and the pipe up to your boundary.
Who owns the external stop tap?
CCW says the water company is responsible for the external stop tap, which is usually in the public footpath. Your internal stop tap, inside the home, is yours.
Who pays for a shared supply pipe?
Where one pipe serves more than one property, CCW says it is the joint responsibility of all the owners connected to it. Agree a split with your neighbours before work starts.
I rent. Who fixes a leaking pipe?
Thames Water says that tenants should tell their landlord or agent, who is responsible for fixing leaks. Report it in writing and keep a copy.
