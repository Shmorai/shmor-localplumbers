What the plumber typically does

  • First fix: running new supply and waste pipes before walls and floors are closed up.
  • Second fix: connecting sinks, toilets, showers, baths, appliances and fittings.
  • Checking water pressure and flow suit the shower or fittings you've chosen.
  • Testing for leaks and making sure waste pipes have the right fall.

Rules and permits

UK: some work must meet Building Regulations (building standards in Scotland), for example new drainage, some electrical work in bathrooms and unvented hot water cylinders. Water fittings must meet water regulations. A WaterSafe approved plumber can certify certain work.

USA: remodels that move or add plumbing usually need a permit and inspection under your local plumbing code. Your licensed plumber or general contractor normally handles this.

Getting comparable quotes

  • Share the same drawings or list of fittings with every plumber.
  • Ask what's included: removal of old fittings, making good, tiling, electrics, waste disposal.
  • Check who supplies the fittings, and who is responsible if a part is faulty.
  • Agree a payment schedule tied to stages of work, not a large upfront payment.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a plumber or a bathroom fitter?
Many bathroom fitters do plumbing, tiling and carpentry. For bigger jobs or moving pipes and drains, make sure whoever does the plumbing is suitably registered (UK) or licensed (USA).
Can I buy my own fittings?
Often, yes, but agree it with your plumber first. Some won't guarantee work with customer-supplied parts.
