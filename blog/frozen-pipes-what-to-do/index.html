Frozen pipes: what to do, and when to call a plumber

A hard freeze can leave you with no water, or a burst pipe once things thaw. Acting quickly and safely limits the damage. Here is what the UK Met Office and the American Red Cross advise.

By the LocalPlumbers editorial team · 19 November 2026 · 3 min read

First, find your stop tap or main shut-off

Whatever happens, you need to be able to stop the water fast. In the UK this is the stop tap, usually under the kitchen sink but sometimes in a downstairs bathroom, garage, cellar or under the stairs. The Met Office suggests checking it works every six months. In the US it is the main water shut-off valve, and FEMA advises knowing where the valves are before an emergency.

Do it now, not when water is coming through the ceiling.

How to prevent frozen pipes

  • Insulate pipes in lofts, garages, cellars and other cold spaces, and have your boiler serviced (Met Office).
  • Keep heating on low if you go away, and ask a neighbour to look in now and then (Met Office).
  • Open cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks so warmer room air can reach the pipes (American Red Cross).
  • Let a tap trickle during a deep freeze in the US: the Red Cross says even a trickle of running water helps stop pipes freezing. This wastes water, so only do it when a hard freeze is forecast.

If a pipe is frozen but not burst

  1. Turn off the stop tap straight away.
  2. Open all taps to drain the system, keeping a bucket of water for flushing and washing, then close them when the flow stops.
  3. Thaw the pipe slowly with hot water bottles or towels soaked in hot water.
  4. Never use a naked flame or blowtorch. The Met Office warns it can permanently damage pipes and cause a fire.
  5. Call a plumber if you cannot find or reach the frozen section.

In the US, the Red Cross suggests thawing with a hair dryer or warm water and also warns against open flames.

If a pipe has burst

  1. Turn off the water at the stop tap or main valve and switch off your boiler (UK advice).
  2. Open all taps to drain the system quickly.
  3. Soak up water with towels to limit damage.
  4. If water has reached electrics, do not touch them and switch off at the fuse box.
  5. Call an approved plumber, and photograph the damage for your insurer.

The CDC's winter weather guidance also says to shut off the main water valve immediately if a pipe bursts. See our guide to leaks and burst pipes and the emergency plumbers page for what to expect.

Choosing the right plumber

In the UK, the Met Office recommends a WaterSafe-approved plumber, which you can find through watersafe.org.uk or on 0333 207 9030. Note down a number before winter and keep it near the boiler or stop tap. For gas appliances you also need a Gas Safe registered engineer. In the US, check your state or city licensing lookup and insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use the heating to thaw a frozen pipe?
No. Water in a frozen pipe has nowhere to go, so the Met Office advises thawing it gently with hot water bottles or warm towels, never with a naked flame or blowtorch.
Where is my stop tap?
Usually under the kitchen sink, but it can be in a downstairs bathroom, kitchen cupboard, garage, cellar or under the stairs. Test it every six months.
Do I need to call a plumber for a burst pipe?
Yes, once the water is off. The Met Office recommends a WaterSafe-approved plumber for a burst pipe, and you should contact your insurer.
